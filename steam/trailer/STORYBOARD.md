# HEXHOLD trailer: storyboard

Cut 3, 2026-10-15: re-recorded on the arcade look. Same edit as cut 2 — the
pacing was re-timed so every word can be read on first viewing and nothing
about that changed — but the game underneath it is pixel art now, and so is
the director's own layer. 81 seconds, 1920x1080 at 60 fps.

Every shot is a real hand on the real game server, dealt from a stacked deck,
with the spells cast through the engine. Text cards are short, in the game's
own type: Bungee through three hard bands of gold, orange and red with a
four-pixel ink outline, which is how the game sets a headline. (Cut 2 set them
in Cinzel with a four-stop metal ramp and a 26px bloom. Re-recording without
redrawing that would have put two products in one video, with the old one on
top.) The music is an original score rendered from code on a 120 BPM grid; the
sound effects are the game's own synths.

The rule for every beat: a setup the viewer can read, one thing the player
does, and what it gets them, in words that need no poker. Hand names the game
prints (FLUSH, FIVE OF A KIND) may appear, but no beat depends on knowing what
they mean: the text card always says the result in plain words.

The reading rule (Mason, after watching cut 1: "a little too quick to read
anything"): every text card and every line the game puts up stays on screen
at least **1.5 s plus 0.3 s a word**, counted from the moment it has landed
(its slam finished, its letters settled), not from when it began to animate.
Every payoff is held to be read; the moments where cause becomes effect run
in slow motion, with a still beat before the payoff. The table under
"Measured" proves it for this cut.

## What cut 2 changed, and what it left out

Cut 1 (58 s) held most cards for well under two seconds and its banners for
less than the game keeps them up. Holding everything to the reading rule
made it about 92 s, so the two beats that were hardest to read went, rather
than rushing the rest:

- **Cut: THEY CAST SPELLS TOO / CANCEL IT.** The opponent's spell panel and
  your counterspell are paragraphs of in-game text; at a readable pace the
  beat needed nine seconds, and the first fresh-eyes reader had already called
  it "tiny text". The game's spell-on-spell stack is the cost.
- **Cut: OR BUY YOUR OWN / THREE CARDS. EVERY HAND.** The Third Hand card's
  text is small and was flagged as unclear; the new-rule beat now goes straight
  to its payoff.
- **Cut: the TYPE A SPELL insert in the opening.** Typing gets eight unhurried
  seconds of its own right after the title instead of 1.4 rushed ones; the
  opening's spell is still a written one (its card in your hand says
  "copy my king").
- **Moved:** writing a spell now comes straight after the title (16 s), the
  most unusual thing in the game, where every fresh-eyes reader wanted it.

## The 6-second Steam preview loop

`hexhold-preview-6s.mp4` is cut 2's 4.0-9.6 s: two Kings of Spades under
MAKE IT FIVE, then FIVE OF A KIND landing and held; its last 0.4 s dissolves
back into its own first frame so a looping player never jumps. Silent. Six
seconds cannot hold two cards to the rule, so the payoff gets the time: the
banner's FIVE OF A KIND, KINGS is on screen 3.4 s before the dissolve (rule
for five words: 3.0 s); MAKE IT FIVE gets 1.25 s and comes round again on
every loop.

## How it got here: fresh-eyes reads

Each draft went to a reader who had never seen the game, asked what the game
is, what is fun, what confuses, and whether they would wishlist.

- **Draft 1, 7/10.** Got the fun right ("a spell rewrote reality and I won").
  Flagged: peek-and-burn needed poker maths in 1.5 seconds; the opponent's
  spell panel was small text; "7 and 2 beats Aces" only lands for poker
  players; the four finale casts blurred; writing your own spell had 2.5 s.
  Fixed: peek-and-burn cut, the write beat lengthened with a visible result
  per clause, close shots on the showdown rows, a ring and a music hit per
  finale copy.
- **Draft 2, 6/10.** "Write your own spell is the hook." Flagged: the wins
  only land if you know hand rankings; the typed spell came too late. Fixed:
  every payoff says its result in plain words; the write beat moved earlier.
- **Draft 3, 6/10.** Flagged: the spell still buried at 18 s; "Flush" and
  "Ace High" are jargon; the ending echoed the opening. Fixed: the written
  spell makes the opening's Five of a Kind; the undo beat ends on the win;
  the finale is set up as one specific card.
- **Draft 4, 7/10.** "A poker game where you write your own spells in plain
  words and use them to bend the cards, the table and the rules." That is
  the fun, said back. Flagged: a quiet first frame; wanted a plain line
  saying what the player does. Fixed: the first frame is the four Kings with
  the camera moving; the title line is CHEAT AT POKER. WITH MAGIC.
- **Cut 1, watched by Mason:** "a little too quick to read anything." Cut 2
  above.
- **Cut 2, a new reader, asked "could you read everything on screen?":**
  given 28 frames across the cut, full-size frames of the text-heavy moments
  and how long each text stays: "Main captions are large, gold on dark, and
  held well over the rule, so they read easily on a first viewing." It said
  the game back ("poker where you cast magic that bends the rules... make
  cards wild, undo cards, copy cards, write your own spells in plain English"),
  named the fun (the impossible five Kings, typing a spell, winning with the
  worst hand, the count to five) and scored it 7/10. What it could not read
  comfortably, and what changed: the worst-hand lines sat over the last
  results row (now framed with room under the rows); EVERY ROUND, A NEW RULE
  crossed your hand (now at the top); the game's own small print, the banners'
  second lines and the typed sentence seen from the full page (banners framed
  closer; the Blank Page framed on the sentence and its reading, the typed
  words now about 45 px tall); the end card's small print (larger and
  brighter). It also asked for a beat teaching the rules of poker; the trailer
  keeps its rule that no beat needs them.

## As filmed (2026-10-11)

`EDIT` in `tools/trailer/record_trailer.mjs` lists the ten shots and their
lengths; `render_music.py` puts its hits on the same seconds.

- **Shots are cut, not played in order.** The Undo hand is played before the
  Market where the sentence is written, and cut in after it.
- **Banners land on the music.** The game's showdown banner slams 0.40 s
  after the last check, measured at 60 fps; the recorder calls the check that
  much early, so FIVE OF A KIND lands at 5.5 s and FLUSH FIVE at 67.5 s.
- **Slow motion** is the game's own clock turned slower while the video runs
  at 60 fps: the 7 becoming a King at 0.35x, the undo at 0.5x, the banners
  held at 0.5-0.7x so the game keeps them up while they are read, the
  worst-hand showdown at 0.55x, the Flush Five slam at 0.34x.
- **Close-ups** are the game re-rendered sharp at up to 2x, never a crop of a
  1080p frame.
- **CANCEL IT is gone, and the COUNTERED stamp with it.** Cut 1 showed the
  counter without the stamp because the game never sends a view with a
  countered spell in it; recorded in `plans/FINDINGS.md` as open.
- **The poster** is 70.4 s: FLUSH FIVE, KINGS over the table, FIVE OF THE
  SAME CARD, two Kings of Spades in your hand.
