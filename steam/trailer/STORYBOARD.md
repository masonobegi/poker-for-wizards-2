# HEXHOLD trailer: storyboard

Draft 5 (final), 2026-10-11. About 58 seconds, 1920x1080 at 60 fps. Every shot is a
real hand on the real game server, dealt from a stacked deck, with the spells
cast through the engine. Captions are short text cards over the game, in the
game's own type (Cinzel, gold). Music is an original score rendered from code,
cut to a 120 BPM grid (one bar = 2 seconds); the sound effects are the game's
own synths.

The rule for every beat: a setup the viewer can read, one thing the player
does, and what it gets them, in words that need no poker. Hand names the game
prints (FLUSH, FOUR OF A KIND) may appear, but no beat depends on knowing what
they mean: the text card always says the result in plain words.

## Pacing

- Hits on the music grid: 4.0 (Five of a Kind), 6.0 (title), 14.0 (their
  spell), 18.0 (the blank page), 27.0 (the new rule), 41.0-44.6 (the four
  copies), 46.0 (Flush Five), 50.0 (end card).
- Slow motion (the game clock at half to quarter speed while the video runs at
  60 fps) only on the impossible moments: the 7 becoming a King and the Flush
  Five slam.
- Camera: close on the cards for every payoff, wider only to show who is at the
  table. Nothing is drawn over the game except the text cards and the gold
  rings that point at a card.

## The 6-second Steam preview loop

Seconds 0.0-6.0: four Kings, "copy my king" typed, the 7 becomes a King,
FIVE OF A KIND. It ends on the banner, which cuts cleanly back to the first
frame.

## How it got here: two fresh-eyes reads

Each draft went to a reader who had never seen the game and saw only this
table, asked what the game is, what is fun, what confuses, and whether they
would wishlist.

- **Draft 1, 7/10.** Got the fun right ("a spell rewrote reality and I won").
  Flagged: peek-and-burn needed poker maths in 1.5 seconds; the opponent's
  spell panel was small text; "7 and 2 beats Aces" only lands for poker
  players; the four finale casts blurred; and writing your own spell, the most
  unusual thing in the game, had 2.5 seconds. Fixed: peek-and-burn cut (the
  written spell does the peeking), the write beat lengthened with a visible
  result per clause, close shots on the spell panel and the showdown rows, a
  ring and a music hit per finale copy.
- **Draft 2, 6/10.** "Write your own spell is the hook; I'd try a silly one
  first." Flagged: the wins only land if you know hand rankings (why are five
  Kings special? why is the worst hand winning a surprise?); the typed spell
  came too late (24 s). Fixed: every payoff now says its result in plain words
  (EVERY DECK HAS FOUR / MAKE A FIFTH, YOU HAD NOTHING. YOU WON, THERE IS ONE
  KING OF SPADES); the write beat moved to 17.5 s; the shop's two halves
  (rule change, purchase) and their payoff share one hand, which bought the
  time.
- **Draft 3, 6/10.** "The typed custom spell is the most original thing
  here; I'd try writing my own first." Flagged: it is still buried at 18 s;
  "Flush" and "Ace High" are jargon; the opening and the ending felt like the
  same beat. Fixed: the trailer now opens on a spell being typed ("copy my
  king") and that written spell makes the Five of a Kind, so the hook is the
  game's most unusual verb and Mason's "a card splits, now it's Five of a
  Kind" at once; the undo beat ends on the win, not on a hand name; the
  finale is set up as one specific card (THERE IS ONE KING OF SPADES) so it
  reads as further than the opening, not the same.
- **Draft 4, 7/10.** "A poker game where you write your own spells in plain
  words and use them to bend the cards, the table and the rules. You try to
  win hands by breaking poker." That is the fun, said back. Flagged: the first
  two seconds were a quiet blank page; it wanted a plain line saying what the
  player does. Fixed in draft 5: the first frame is the four Kings with the
  camera already moving, the typing is a 1.4-second insert, and the title
  line is CHEAT AT POKER. WITH MAGIC.

## As filmed (2026-10-11)

The cut matches the table above to the frame: `EDIT` in
`tools/trailer/record_trailer.mjs` lists the fourteen shots and their lengths,
and `render_music.py` puts its hits on the same seconds. What changed on the
way from paper to picture, from watching the frames:

- **CANCEL IT shows the counter, not a stamp.** The game never sends a view
  with a countered spell in it (the stack resolves and clears in one step), so
  the COUNTERED stamp cannot appear in real play; the shot shows what a player
  sees: their Burn, your Nullify on top of it, the blue flash. Recorded in
  `plans/FINDINGS.md` as open.
- **Banners land on the music.** The game's showdown banner slams 0.40 s
  after the last check, measured at 60 fps; the recorder calls the check that
  much early, so FIVE OF A KIND lands at 4.0 s and FLUSH FIVE at 46.0 s.
- **The finale's slow motion** runs the game at a third of real speed from
  45.85 s to 47.4 s; the 7 becoming a King runs at 0.4x.
- **Close-ups** are the game re-rendered sharp at up to 2x, never a crop of a
  1080p frame.
- **The poster** is 48.6 s: FLUSH FIVE, KINGS over the table, two Kings of
  Spades in your hand.
